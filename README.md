# Narra - Your voice, your story!

A responsive English-learning app built from the supplied screenshots, walkthrough videos, Narra brand board and UAT sheet. The current app uses four tabs: **Lesson, Practice, Dictionary and Profile**, with English/Vietnamese guidance, rounded Quicksand headings, Nunito body text and the Narra lime/cyan identity. Its document title is exactly **Narra - Your voice, your story!**

Built with React, TypeScript, Vite, Three.js, Lucide, `qrcode` and Vercel Blob. Learning preferences and demo community choices stay in this browser. Shared speaking challenges have a small private server-backed room; account authentication and live AI tutoring are not connected.

## Run locally

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open [the local app](http://localhost:5174). `npm run build` runs TypeScript and creates the production build in `dist/`.

## What is real, local or simulated

Browser speech recognition captures English transcripts when the browser supports `SpeechRecognition`/`webkitSpeechRecognition`; text-to-speech uses available browser voices. A learner chooses a demo, enters a speaking session, taps replay or starts the mic. Camera access is a separate explicit control and is off by default. Subsequent tutor replies can speak automatically within that user-started session when sound is enabled. Microphone/camera permission, speech-service availability and autoplay policies vary by device. A browser may use its vendor's network service for recognition, so this is not an offline or entirely on-device speech-processing guarantee.

The video-style tutor is a locally rendered rigged 3D character with procedural idle, gesture, listening and approximate mouth movement. The central speech controller uses real browser playback start/end/boundary events and cancels conflicting playback/recognition. This is not a human WebRTC call, generated human video or phoneme-accurate lip-sync. The optional learner camera is a local preview, not a transmitted video stream. Browser TTS is not a dedicated neural tutor voice service; actual voices and accents depend on what the browser provides.

Say My Words and Pronunciation can record and play back microphone audio with `MediaRecorder`. Recordings are temporary object URLs while that practice screen remains open. Attempt counts and progress are saved locally; audio is not uploaded by the app or retained as a cloud recording.

Tutor responses, hints, translations and language suggestions are deterministic samples. The app does not run a live language model, evaluate phonemes, or assign a pronunciation score from recognition confidence or typed text. Conversation detail can show observed turn duration and transcript-based words per minute; those are not pronunciation or proficiency scores. Shared challenge counts are browser-reported metrics, not an anti-cheat system. Its automatic task check is only a word-count target, not semantic task assessment. Writing Check reports counts and simple local practice notes, without an AI grade or estimated level. Dictionary IELTS-practice examples are Narra-authored teaching examples, not official IELTS questions, answers or level assessments.

Initial progress includes reference sample values such as 11 minutes and one conversation. These seeded values can affect the initial XP/achievement display; they are not measurements collected from a new learner. Fictional league users, feed posts and province comparison figures are labeled as demo content. A profile QR encodes a real invitation URL, but public profile identity, cross-device friend requests, authentication, cloud sync, social messaging, push notifications, scheduled reminders and payments are not connected.

## Storage

Validated `localStorage` records preserve learning preferences, words, progress, conversation drafts, avatar appearance, local social choices and plan interest. The intro's once-per-session flag uses `sessionStorage`. Clearing browser storage resets this device's learning state. There is no account database, analytics integration or learning-progress synchronization across devices.

Shared challenges use `api/challenges.ts` and a **private Vercel Blob store**. Configure server-only `CHALLENGE_SIGNING_SECRET` with at least 32 random characters and either `BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN`, or Vercel OIDC with `BLOB_STORE_ID`. Never expose these values through `VITE_` variables or the client bundle. The challenge API is a Vercel function; plain `npm run dev` serves the client but does not emulate that function. Use a Vercel environment for end-to-end room testing.

Room IDs are random UUIDs. Participant passes are HMAC-signed and retained in that participant's browser; passes are not part of the shared invitation URL. Immutable room, participant, start and result objects avoid simultaneous players overwriting each other. Origin reads bypass Blob's CDN cache. The server validates input, limits room size to two and enforces expiry; writes use Blob's automatic one-day TTL, whose deletion is evaluated daily. Access expires after 24 hours even if physical object deletion happens later.

Only names, room configuration, timestamps and result metrics are stored in the room. Narra does not upload or retain challenge audio or transcripts. Extra POST fields such as `transcript` are ignored rather than persisted. Share/copy actions are explicit; selecting a fictional friend does not join that character, send a message or fabricate participation. Anyone who receives the unguessable invitation can view/join its available slot, so share it with the intended person.

## Visual system and assets

The supplied wordmark and speech-bubble “a” artwork live in `public/brand/`; the tagline is **Your voice, your story!** App microcopy connects related ideas with commas and natural conjunctions rather than a series of short sentences, in both English and Vietnamese. Use commas or the standard hyphen (-) instead of long dashes in user-facing app copy. Teaching examples retain the sentence punctuation needed for learning. The primary palette is lime `#9CE943`, cyan `#6DD9FF`, orange `#FF6E0B`, black and light surfaces. Lime controls use dark text, with a readable derived green for small accents. Dark appearance uses deep blue-green surfaces while retaining the same brand accents.

Quicksand is the display font; Nunito is the body/control font. Both are served locally, with their OFL notices in `public/quicksand-OFL.txt` and `public/nunito-OFL.txt`. The original wordmark remains artwork rather than a replacement typeface. Source and generated visual assets are documented in [brand guidelines](docs/brand-guidelines.md), `public/brand/asset-provenance.json` and `tools/asset-prompts.md`.

The chapter order, lesson names and tutor identities are recorded in `src/data.ts`. Reference assets and sampled walkthrough frames are retained under `tools/` and `public/assets/`. Province geometry comes from [TaliaCao/Vietnam-Shape-Map](https://github.com/TaliaCao/Vietnam-Shape-Map); its MIT notice is bundled in `public/assets/vietnam-map-LICENSE.txt`.

## Two brand versions

The original lime/cyan version and the additional Option 5 ribbon identity share the same lessons and features. Build Option 5 with `VITE_BRAND_VARIANT=option5`; without it, the original identity remains active. Local visual review can use `/?brand=option5`. Logo, icon and Buddy paths are selected centrally in `src/brand-variant.ts`.

The original Narra intro uses an articulated blue Buddy: shy tiptoe steps, a glance left and right, then a second Buddy in a cap and orange scarf dashes in, takes Buddy’s hand and leads a run off screen. The logo and exact English slogan `Your voice, your story` appear afterwards. This sequence lasts 6.8 seconds.

Option 5 retains its original image-based caterpillar Buddy. The caterpillar crawls from left to right, turns toward the learner, winks, then reveals `Your voice, your story`. This sequence lasts 3.5 seconds and does not use the tiptoe or friend handoff animation. Both versions offer Skip/Escape, a 400ms reduced-motion alternative and replay from Settings. The slogan stays in English. Session flags are separate per brand; only Option 5’s flag changes for the restored caterpillar intro. Theme and VI/EN preferences continue to work in both versions.

Each deployment keeps browser learning data under its own origin. Separate private Blob stores and signing secrets serve invitation rooms for their respective hostnames.


The streak club now has 11 milestones at 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, 270 and 365 days, each with its own badge, card theme and mascot celebration. Streaks use the UTC+7 study calendar, count a date only once, keep yesterday’s run until today ends, ignore invalid/future dates and continue beyond 365 days. Earned milestones and their first achievement dates come from recorded practice history, so a break does not remove them. Milestone celebrations wait until active lessons close and are acknowledged once per device. Streak cards use the correct brand’s winking thumbs-up mascot, name and slogan; users can capture the card or export a 1080×1350 PNG and invoke their device’s share sheet, without automatically posting anywhere. Locked milestones have a persistent preview watermark, including exported images. Word-workout reactions celebrate consecutive correct answers and offer curiosity or encouragement after misses, with no speaking score inferred from recognition confidence.

Streak date and unlock checks: `node --test tools/qa/streaks.test.mjs`.
